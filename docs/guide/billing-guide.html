Billing in the Snelson Server CRM

A practical guide: from contract lines to invoices in Xero, with every screen along the way

Version 2 · October 2026 · Screens taken from the demo data, so names and figures are illustrative.

Xero remains the system of record for issued invoices, payments and balances. The CRM calculates, explains and prepares; it creates draft invoices in Xero and never authorises or sends them.

1. How billing works, in one page

Everything about money sits under one sidebar entry, Billing. Its left-hand navigation is ordered the way the month flows.

SectionWhat it is for
Needs attentionEverything a person should look at, from every check, in one list: unmatched provider accounts, unmapped services, duplicates, count differences, quantity changes to approve, supplier cost changes, stale data, drafts, invoices changed in Xero, supplier bills and renewals. Filter by group or customer; every row opens where it is dealt with.
CustomersOne row per customer: agreements, next invoice, charge per month, supplier cost, margin, how many lines the integrations supply, and whether anything needs attention.

ServicesThe service register: every subscription, hosting item and device the integrations report, with the supplier product, quantity, cost, last sync and the line that bills it.
Renewals & pricingThe renewal queue with supplier commitment exposure, and the price review tool.
InvoicesThe Xero sales mirror (outstanding, overdue, paid) and the Pax8 supplier bill reconciliation.
AutomationThe staged automation policy in Settings.

The chain

  1. Contract lines say what a customer pays for: description, quantity, unit price, billing frequency, and for each line an invoice schedule, a reduction policy and a quantity rule (fixed or synced).
  2. Each integration (Pax8, 20i, NinjaOne) reports what it supplies to each customer. A service link ties each supplied service to the contract line that bills it, or records that it is bundled, covered, free or internal. Supplier cost comes through the same link.
  3. The billing engine works out, for a run date, which line periods are due and not yet invoiced and what each costs, including pro-rated additions, credits and catch-ups from dated changes.
  4. The Monthly run shows one row per contract with the proposed amount, the previous invoice and plain-language reasons for any difference. Ticked rows become CRM drafts.
  5. Drafts are reviewed and approved; approval creates the invoice in Xero as a DRAFT with a reference back to the CRM.
  6. Finance approves and sends from Xero. Payments and status changes flow back and appear under Invoices.
  7. Between runs, Needs attention, Services and Renewals keep the next run clean.
Nothing an integration reports becomes a customer charge by itself. A supplied service is billed only through a contract line; a changed count only changes an invoice once a person records (or approves) the dated change. Historical invoices never move when provider data changes.

2. Setting a customer up to be billed

2.1 Link the company to its Xero contact

A draft can be prepared without the link but cannot be approved. Go to Integrations → Xero → Mapping, or follow the red "Link this customer to its Xero contact" action on the company's Billing tab. The link is one to one: a Xero contact can belong to only one CRM company.

2.2 Give the contract the right terms

On the contract (Contracts & Services → the agreement → Edit):

  • Start date and billing frequency anchor the periods. A billing day (1 to 28) anchors them to that day of the month instead; a start part-way through a period is pro-rated.
  • Bill from (CRM) is the billing commencement in the CRM. Periods from this date with no draft are proposed as missed. Leave it empty for a contract imported mid-life so nothing earlier is back-billed.
  • Renewal date and notice period drive the renewal queue; Prices fixed until the renewal date stops a price review changing this customer before then.
  • Customer PO reference is copied onto every invoice drafted from the agreement and sent to Xero on the first line.

2.3 Lines: schedule and reduction policy

SettingMeaning
Invoice schedule: With the contract (default)The line's price is spread over the contract's invoices. An annual £120 domain on a monthly contract bills £10 a month.
Invoice schedule: On its own cycleThe line is invoiced once per its own period, anchored like the contract. A domain renewed yearly is billed once a year, in full.
Reduction policy: Next period (default)A lower quantity applies from the next period; nothing is credited inside the current one.
Reduction policy: ImmediateThe unused days are credited as a negative line (and as a catch-up if the period was already invoiced).
Reduction policy: At renewalThe old quantity is billed until the contract's renewal date.
Quantity rule: Fixed (default)The agreed quantity bills. If Pax8, 20i or NinjaOne supply a different count, that is an exception for a person to decide (amend, reduce at renewal, bundle, accept, dismiss).
Quantity rule: SyncedThe integrations' count is what should be billed. A different count is a quantity change to approve; one approval records it as a dated change from the day it changed, and the next invoice pro-rates it. Nothing changes until approved.

2.4 Say which line bills each supplied service

On the company's Billing tab, each agreement line shows Supplied by: which integration supplies it and how many. Services the integrations report that no line accounts for are listed below the agreements under Supplied but not on an active agreement, unmapped ones first. For each, pick the line in Billed on, or use Not charged? to record that it is bundled into another line, covered by a commitment, intentionally free (with a review date) or internal.

Matching by rule happens at sync time and is recorded, so you can see how a service came to be on a line: chosen by a person, matched by catalogue SKU, matched by product name, or the per-device rule (lines marked "compare with NinjaOne" take the billable device count). A person's choice is never overwritten by a sync.

3. The monthly run: Prepare, Approve, Issued

The run is safe to repeat. A line period that already has a draft is never proposed again, whichever draft carries it.

Step 1: Prepare

Billing → Monthly run → Prepare. Pick a run date (today by default). The summary line says how many contracts are ready, need review or are blocked, the expected billing total, potential missed revenue across unmapped services, and renewals and notice deadlines this week.

Each row shows the customer and agreement, the period, the proposed net, the previous comparable draft (linked), the difference, and a status:

  • Ready: Xero linked, same amount as the previous invoice, nothing open for the customer. Ticked by default.
  • Needs review: something deserves a look: a changed amount with its reasons, an open discrepancy, an unmapped service, Pax8 or NinjaOne data not fetched for three hours, missed periods, or a first invoice from the CRM. Tick after reading the reasons.
  • Blocked: the company has no Xero link, so the draft could not be approved. Cannot be ticked.

Open the why toggle on a row to see the reasons. They are read from the calculated lines against the previous draft, never guessed: "Users: 10 → 12", "price £40.00 → £42.00 per unit", "2 added from 14 Oct, pro rata (£10.92)", "Includes 2 missed periods".

Leave One draft per customer ticked to get a single consolidated draft when several agreements of the same customer are due. Click Prepare drafts.

Step 2: Approve

Billing → Monthly run → Approve lists every draft awaiting approval with its verdict:

  • Unchanged: same contract lines, quantities and prices as the previous invoice, Xero linked, not stale, a plain draft. Ticked by default; Approve N in Xero creates them one after another. Each is re-checked at the moment of approval and anything that changed meanwhile is skipped with the reason.
  • Needs review: the amount or lines differ (with the why reasons), the draft is stale (the contract changed after it was prepared), it is the first invoice from the CRM, or it was prepared by hand. Open it with Review & approve.
  • Failed: Xero rejected it; the error is shown. Fix and approve again.

Step 3: Issued

Billing → Monthly run → Issued lists what was created in Xero, with the Xero invoice number and a link to each customer schedule. If an invoice is changed, voided or deleted in Xero after approval, it appears at the top against the approved version. From here on, approve and send from Xero; payments come back through the hourly sync and webhooks.

4. Reading a draft and the customer schedule

A draft page has the lines (editable until approval), What the customer sees (one paragraph derived from the calculated lines), How these amounts were calculated (one explanation per line: the quantity on the first day, each addition for its remaining days, credits, catch-ups), the details (dates, period, PO reference, Xero contact) and the approval controls.

If the contract changed after the draft was prepared, a stale notice lists the changes since and offers Re-prepare from the contract: the old draft is cancelled, its periods and changes are handed back, and a fresh draft is prepared for the same stretch.

The customer schedule

Click Customer schedule on a draft for a printable page to send with the Xero invoice: the explanation, the charges grouped by agreement with service dates, the supporting list of licences, devices and domains behind each charge, the PO reference and the agreement's renewal date. Use Print or save as PDF.

Preparing one invoice by hand

On a contract page, Prepare invoice opens a dialog with the period. The calculated lines, net and any already-drafted periods are shown before anything is created, so a partial period or a double-up is visible up front. A hand-typed period that is not an anchored one is pro-rated like any other partial period.

5. Handling changes during a period

Every quantity or price change on an active contract is recorded as dated history: the value before and after, the effective day, who made it and why, and the draft that first accounted for it. The engine always calculates from this history, so several changes in one period are each charged for their own days.

How to record a change

  1. Open the contract and click Edit.
  2. Change the quantity or unit price on the line.
  3. Set Changes take effect from to the day the change applies (the day the users started, not the day you heard). Add a reason.
  4. Save. The contract page shows the pending change on the line and the Change history card.
ChangeWhat the next invoice does
Quantity up, inside the current periodThe period is charged at the quantity on its first day, plus the extra units for their remaining days, pro rata. If the period was already invoiced, the difference is caught up on the next invoice.
Quantity downFollows the line's reduction policy: from the next period, credited now, or at renewal.
Price changeApplies from the next anchored period starting on or after the effective day. Price changes are never pro-rated.
Backdated changeA change dated inside the previous period produces a traceable catch-up line: what that period should have cost, what was invoiced, the difference.
Cancelling a draft never loses a change: its periods become due again and its changes wait for the next draft.

Resolving a count difference

Every row opens the customer's Billing tab with the line expanded. The line shows the agreed and supplied counts and a Resolve… button with the options and the money consequence before anything happens:

  • Amend the contract line to the supplied count from a chosen date: a dated change with a reason, pro-rated by the engine, linked back from the item.
  • Reduce at renewal (fewer supplied): the lower count, billed at the agreed count until the renewal date.
  • Include in a bundle (licences): the chosen subscriptions count toward another line instead.
  • Accept as an exception with an owner, a review date and a reason. It re-opens by itself after the date, or earlier if the gap grows. Accepted exceptions have their own view on Needs attention.
  • Dismiss: not a billing matter.

7. Services: what the integrations supply, and what bills it

Billing → Services lists every service the integrations say a customer has: the integration, the supplier product (SKU, package type, device classes), the quantity and cost last synced, when it was synced, its state and the line that bills it, with how that link came about. Filter by state, integration or customer. Only unmapped may mean missed revenue; every other state is a recorded decision with a reason or a rule match.

StateMeaning
Charged on a lineA contract line bills it. The register says how: chosen by a person, matched by catalogue SKU, matched by product name, or the per-device rule.
Included in a bundleAnother line pays for it; its quantity counts toward that line in the count check.
Covered by commitmentPart of a commitment already billed; counts toward nothing.
Intentionally freeDeliberately free, with a reason and a review date. Comes back to Needs attention when the date passes.
Internal / non-billableOur own use; left out of counts.
Needs investigationParked with a reason until someone decides.
UnmappedNo decision and no rule match. Potential missed revenue.

Cost is shown where the supplier prices the service (Pax8). 20i and NinjaOne carry no pricing, so their cost reads not reported, never zero; record a unit cost on the line if you know it, and the line's margin uses that.

Adding another integration later means one adapter that reports services in this shape; the register, the checks and every screen then work the same way for it.

8. Renewals and price reviews

Renewals

Billing → Renewals & pricing → Renewals queues every active contract with a renewal date by its decision deadline: a configurable lead (Settings → General → Renewal decision lead, 30 days by default) before the notice deadline. Each row sets the customer's renewal against the supplier commitments behind its lines, read from the register, and names the mismatches: "the agreement renews on 31 December but the supplier commitment runs to 30 September (≈ £300 for 2 months beyond it)" or "the supplier commitment renews before the agreement; the cost may change first".

  • Record decision: renew as is, renew with amendments, or not renewing (with a reason). Kept against that renewal date and audited; cleared if the date moves.
  • Prepare amendment: opens the contract with "Changes take effect from" set to the renewal date, so the amendment lands as dated history and the invoice preview shows its effect first.

Price reviews

Billing → Renewals & pricing → Price review. Choose a scope (a catalogue product, or lines whose description contains a phrase), a change (percentage, new unit price, or a supplier cost passed through at the same margin), an effective date and a reason, then Preview. Every affected line is listed with price and cost now and after, margin now and after, the monthly change, the day it applies for that customer, the first invoice period that carries it, and the constraints (prices fixed until renewal, agreement ending first, customer not renewing, notice deadline near). Untick anything to leave out, then Apply. Each change is a dated, reasoned entry in the contract's history; no price changes silently.

9. Invoices and supplier bills

Customer invoices

Billing → Invoices mirrors the Xero sales invoices: outstanding, overdue, paid in the last 30 days, drafts in Xero, and the list with status, dates, totals and balances. Figures are cached from the hourly sync and webhooks; Refresh from Xero pulls now. Live balances per customer are on the company page.

Supplier bills (Pax8)

The Supplier bills tab compares the Pax8 partner invoices with the purchase bills entered in Xero for the Pax8 supplier contact. Matches are made by reference (strong) or by a unique identical total within ten days (weaker, amber); finance can match or unmatch by hand and a hand decision is kept. Each Pax8 invoice's charge lines are allocated to customers and subscriptions, so an equal total cannot hide a line-level difference. Import invoices from a date brings older history in. Nothing is changed in Xero or at Pax8.

10. Automation, in stages

Settings → Billing automation holds a three-level policy. It only ever applies the same rules a person applies on the Monthly run and the draft review, and every run is audited naming the policy.

LevelWhat runs by itselfWhat stays with people
0 Detect only (default)Findings, the workspace, the draft review and the renewal queue are kept current.Everything else.
1 Prepare ready draftsOn the run day each month, drafts are prepared for contracts rated ready.Review and approval; every review or blocked contract.
2 Prepare, then approve unchangedAs 1, then drafts the review finds unchanged are created in Xero as drafts.Every exception; authorising and sending in Xero.

The scheduled job runs daily at 07:00 and acts once a month on or after the run day. Run the policy now runs it by hand. The last run's summary is shown on the page.

11. One customer at a glance: the company Billing tab and the Customers list

Every company page has one Billing tab. It answers, on one screen: what are we billing this customer, what does each service cost us, which integration or manual entry supplies it, does the billed quantity match the rule, what changed, and what will the next invoice be.

  • The strip: charge per month, supplier cost per month (with how many lines have an unknown cost), margin, the next invoice date, the Xero balance and the number of things needing attention.
  • Needs attention for this customer only.
  • One card per agreement: frequency, the current period, the next invoice date, the renewal date and what the next run proposes. Each line shows the billed quantity and its rule, Supplied by, unit price, cost per unit (from the supplier where it prices the service, otherwise the recorded cost, otherwise unknown), margin per month and a status: in step, count differs, quantity change to approve, exception accepted, recorded cost differs from supplier, or no integration supplies this.
  • Open a line for the source records (product, quantity, cost, last sync, how it matched, a link to the supplier console) with the line picker, the rule in plain words, pending dated changes and the count check with Resolve.
  • Supplied but not on an active agreement: unmapped services first, each with the line picker and the coverage dialog.
  • Drafts awaiting approval, then collapsed sections for Invoices (balances, history, drafts created from the CRM), Count checks and renewals, and Supplier detail (the Pax8 and 20i panels, including partner invoices and quantity changes at Pax8).

The Customers list

Billing → Customers is the same picture one row per customer: agreements and frequency, next invoice, charge, supplier cost (unknown where no supplier prices it), margin, how many lines the integrations supply and an attention count. Filter to customers needing attention, with an unknown cost, or supplied without an agreement. Click through to the customer's Billing tab.

From a signed proposal to a contract

When a Better Proposals proposal is signed, the CRM normally drafts the contract itself from the opportunity's lines. If it did not (the proposal was built outside the CRM, or was linked later), the Proposals page shows Create contract on every signed proposal without one. With a linked opportunity the full acceptance runs; with only a company linked, a draft contract is created from the proposal's totals for you to replace with the agreed services before activating.

13. Quick answers

A customer added two users on the 14th. What do I do? Edit the contract, change the quantity, set "Changes take effect from" to the 14th, save. The next run charges the two for their remaining days and the full quantity from the next period. The customer explanation will say so in plain words.

The licence check says agreed 14, Pax8 supplies 16. Open Needs attention (or the company's Billing tab), open the line, click Resolve, choose Amend the contract line from the day the licences were added, and apply. The item closes and the next invoice carries the change. If that line should always follow Pax8, set its quantity rule to synced: future differences arrive as a quantity change to approve.

Where does this charge come from? Company → Billing, open the line. Supplied by names the integration and the count; the opened line lists each source record with its supplier product, last sync and how it was matched, or says the quantity was entered by hand.

Why is the cost "unknown"? Only Pax8 reports prices. 20i and NinjaOne do not, so lines they supply use the cost recorded on the line, and read unknown until one is recorded. The CRM never invents a cost.

Pax8 changed a price. Needs attention shows "supplier cost differs from the recorded cost" for the line; margin already uses the new figure. Update the recorded cost on the line, then run a price review if the sell price should move.

A draft says "stale". The contract changed after it was prepared. Open it and click Re-prepare from the contract.

Two agreements for one customer are due. Can they go on one invoice? Yes: leave One draft per customer ticked in step 1. The draft groups the lines by agreement and covers both.

Why is a contract "blocked"? Its company has no Xero contact link. Link it on Integrations → Xero and run again.

A supplier commitment runs past the customer's renewal. It shows in Renewals as a mismatch with the months and cost at stake. Decide before the decision deadline; if the customer leaves, that cost stays payable.

Can the CRM send an invoice? No. It creates drafts in Xero; approving, sending and payments stay in Xero.

What does "potential missed revenue" mean? The partner cost of services nobody has mapped to a line. It is not confirmed billable until someone decides; confirmed gaps (expected charges not drafted, covered services that cannot charge) are listed separately.
